Robotic Test Automation for Automotive Door-Handle Validation

Development and validation of a robotic test platform for repeatable automotive door-handle testing, progressing from a Braccio proof of concept with a self-developed closed-loop feedback architecture toward integration with an industrial-grade robotic platform.

Robotics Integration Embedded C/C++ CAN / CANoe CAPL Test & Validation Sensor Interaction
PROJECT OVERVIEW

Building a more repeatable way to validate automotive door-handle functions

The work focused on developing a robotic approach for repetitive automotive door-handle validation. The objective was not only to automate the physical movement, but also to connect the robotic action with the ECU response so that the system could determine whether the intended interaction had been detected.

The development was approached in stages. A Braccio robotic arm was first used as a proof of concept to establish the closed-loop testing concept. The validated approach was then extended toward an industrial-grade six-degree-of-freedom robotic platform.

6-DOF Robotic platform

THE ENGINEERING CHALLENGE

Physical movement alone was not enough

A robotic arm can reproduce a physical movement, but reliable validation requires more than simply commanding the robot to move to a predefined position.

The robotic action had to be connected to the actual ECU response. The system therefore needed a feedback path that could determine whether the intended sensor interaction had been detected and allow the test sequence to react to that result.

01 Execute robotic action
02 Monitor ECU response
03 Evaluate detection
04 Feed result back
PHASE 01 · PROOF OF CONCEPT

Creating a closed-loop test system around the Braccio

The Braccio six-degree-of-freedom arm was used as the initial proof-of-concept platform. Its purpose was to establish whether robotic motion could be combined with ECU feedback to perform a repeatable validation sequence.

Since the proof-of-concept setup did not provide the complete feedback behaviour required for the test, the closed-loop logic was developed around the robotic controller and the vehicle communication interface.

HARDWARE

Control & communication

  • Braccio 6-DOF robotic arm
  • Arduino GIGA R1 controller
  • C/C++ firmware
  • TJA1051 CAN transceiver
VALIDATION

Feedback architecture

  • CANoe-based ECU monitoring
  • CAPL-based detection logic
  • CAN feedback to the controller
  • Automatic retry behaviour
Closed-loop sequence

Robot executes the action → ECU response is monitored in CANoe → CAPL evaluates the response → detection status is transmitted back to the controller → the controller reacts to the result.

PROOF-OF-CONCEPT RESULT

From commanded movement to feedback-driven testing

The proof of concept demonstrated that the robotic action could be linked to the ECU response instead of operating as an isolated motion sequence.

Approach and Unlock actions were automated, a repeatability and reproducibility study was completed, and ECU-state monitoring was integrated through CANoe.

END-EFFECTOR INVESTIGATION

Investigating the material interaction behind reliable sensor triggering

The robotic movement itself was only one part of the problem. The end-effector had to interact with the capacitive sensing area in a way that produced reliable detection.

An experimental investigation was therefore carried out to compare different probe and material configurations. The testing considered material behaviour together with factors such as applied force, temperature and grounding.

INVESTIGATION

What was evaluated

  • End-effector probe materials
  • Conductive configurations
  • Force-dependent behaviour
  • Temperature-dependent behaviour
  • Grounding configuration
OBSERVATION

Comparing sensor response

The measurements were used to compare the consistency of sensor triggering across the tested configurations rather than treating the end-effector as a purely mechanical component.

Experimental finding

Among the tested configurations, the Copper Plate + NFC Antenna + Foam grounded arrangement showed the most consistent behaviour in the recorded measurements. Other configurations showed different levels of sensor response and sensitivity to the test conditions.

The complete experimental dataset and detailed internal implementation information are not published here.

PHASE 02 · INDUSTRIAL PLATFORM

Extending the validated concept toward Arctos

Once the proof-of-concept approach had been established, the work progressed toward an industrial-grade six-axis robotic platform.

Unlike the Braccio proof of concept, the Arctos platform provides its own encoder-based closed-loop motor architecture. The focus therefore shifted from creating the basic feedback behaviour around the robot to integrating the validated test concept with a more capable robotic platform.

ENGINEERING TAKEAWAY

Turning robotic motion into feedback-driven validation

The main outcome of the work was not simply the integration of a robotic arm. It was the development of a validation approach in which robotic movement, ECU behaviour and test logic could work together as a closed-loop system.

Starting with a proof of concept made it possible to validate the approach before extending it toward a more capable industrial robotic platform.
