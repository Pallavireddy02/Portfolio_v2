WHO I AM

Mindset

I enjoy working on systems where hardware, software and mechanics have to work together. What excites me most isn't building something from scratch, it's understanding why something isn't working and staying with the problem until it finally does.

During my master's thesis, I assembled Robotic hardware, Integrated embedded software, validated communication between components and debugged failures across the complete system. Those moments when a small observation solved what looked like a complex problem taught me more than any textbook ever could.

I'm still early in my engineering journey, but I'm curious enough to explore unfamiliar systems, patient enough to troubleshoot them, and practical enough to keep improving until the solution becomes reliable.

FIRST PRINCIPLES

Break complex problems into simple engineering fundamentals before searching for advanced solutions.

SYSTEM THINKING

Hardware, firmware, communication protocols and mechanics are parts of one system, not separate disciplines.

RELIABILITY

A solution is complete only when someone else can trust it repeatedly.

PROCESS

Workflow

My approach usually begins with understanding how a system behaves before trying to improve it. Whether it's hardware, embedded software or robotics integration, I follow a structured engineering process rather than jumping straight to solutions.

01

Understand

Study the complete system, identify constraints and understand how every component interacts.

02

Break Down

Split large engineering problems into smaller modules that can be tested independently.

03

Build & Integrate

Assemble hardware, integrate embedded software and connect subsystems into one working solution.

04

Validate

Test behaviour, investigate failures, verify communication and improve reliability through iteration.

Key Contributions
  • Analysed the department's existing 3-axis Lock/Unlock tester (LabVIEW, NI DAQ, PCAN, CAN/LIN/K-Line) and identified its limits: three axes, complex fixtures and unreliable capacitive triggering.
  • Built a closed-loop test on a Braccio arm: C/C++ firmware on an Arduino GIGA R1 with a TJA1051 CAN transceiver, plus CAPL commands added to the existing handle library, so CANoe evaluates the sensor response in a time window and the arm retries up to twice. Approach and Unlock now run automatically; R&R study completed.
  • Compared six end-effector probe configurations at 1–5 N, room temperature, −40 °C and +80 °C, grounded and ungrounded, against human-finger reference measurements; copper plate + NFC antenna + foam matched best.
  • Assembled a 3D-printed 6-axis Arctos arm from a kit: integrated joints J1–J5 with servo drives and magnetic encoders, verified target replay on four axes, and fixed planetary-gear binding by reprinting the gears at 96 % scale.
  • Flashed handle ECUs after OEM software updates and, alongside the thesis, ran climatic validation tests with a validation engineer, checking Lock, Unlock, push and pull at 60 °C and recording error values per handle.
